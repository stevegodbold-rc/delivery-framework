# Stage 1: Sales to Delivery Transition

## Purpose

Ensure that everything learned during the sales process is captured and handed to the delivery team before work begins. Customer confidence is at its highest at the point of signature. This stage is about not wasting it.

---

## Entry Criteria

Stage 1 is triggered when a deal reaches contract signature or a formal letter of engagement is issued.

- A signed contract, SOW, or letter of engagement is in place
- The Sales Lead has confirmed the deal is closed
- A scheduled handover date has been agreed between sales and delivery

---

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Sales Lead | Delivers the handover briefing; remains available for context throughout Stage 1 |
| Delivery Owner | Accepts the engagement, reviews the SOW, raises concerns before kickoff |
| Practice Lead | Confirms resource availability and team composition; on threshold deals, co-signs solution design (see Solution Design Accountability on Threshold Deals) |
| Finance Partner | Confirms billing setup, revenue schedule, and PO reference |

---

## Key Activities

**Transition meeting (required).** A structured 60-90 minute session between sales and delivery. Its agenda covers: what the client was told, what the client cares about most, who the difficult stakeholders are, what was left out of scope and why, and what would make this engagement a success in the client's eyes. Output: completed Delivery Handover Record.

**Risk identification.** Any concern raised by the Delivery Owner or Practice Lead during transition is logged. These are inputs to the first client conversation, not blockers to starting.

**Deal Quality at Handover score.** Alongside the SOW review and risk identification, the Delivery Owner rates deal quality across four dimensions, each Green / Amber / Red with a brief written rationale: scope clarity (are boundaries and exclusions unambiguous), commercial position (does the price reflect the delivery team's own estimate of cost to deliver, including whether delivery risk was priced), risk allocation (are the contractual risk terms ones delivery can operate under), and expectation accuracy (does what the client was told match what the SOW says). The score is recorded in the Delivery Handover Record.

This is not a judgement of the Sales Lead's performance on any single deal — a single deal has context that a point-in-time score cannot capture. It is portfolio-level calibration data, consistent with this framework's treatment of closure measures elsewhere: it signals something about organisational process (deal quality by type, by sales lead, over time), not about individual performance on one deal. See delivery portfolio management document for how it is aggregated.

Where the Delivery Owner rates any Deal Quality at Handover dimension Red, they may attach conditions to acceptance: specific, dated actions such as a scope clarification conversation with the client in the first fortnight, or a margin re-forecast before the first monthly review. Conditions are recorded in the Delivery Handover Record with an owner and a due date. They do not stop work starting — this is a middle path between full acceptance and blocking, not a new blocker. Where the Sales Lead and Delivery Owner disagree on a condition, the Delivery Director decides. A condition unresolved at its due date is treated as a trigger for the fortnightly health check conversation in Stage 2, not a new escalation channel.

**Deal to delivery vehicle profiling.** The Delivery Owner rates each complexity and uncertainty variable (Low / Medium / High) in consultation with the Sales Lead and Practice Lead, and records a composite profile and recommended delivery vehicle.

**Team composition brief.** A one-page summary of who will be on the engagement, their roles, and expected time allocation. Shared with the client at or before kickoff.

## Deal to Delivery Vehicle Profiling Variables

Each variable is rated Low / Medium / High at Gate 1 by the Delivery Owner in consultation with the Sales Lead and Practice Lead.

### Complexity Variables

| Variable | What it measures | Planning implication |
|---|---|---|
| Complexity | Technical and organisational complexity of the work | Higher complexity increases the need for senior practitioners, longer planning cycles, and more structured quality checkpoints |
| Team size | Number of practitioners required | Larger teams require more coordination overhead and increase the importance of leadership capability |
| Criticality | How operationally or strategically critical the outcome is to the client | Higher criticality raises the consequences of delay or failure; typically requires closer client engagement and more frequent health reviews |
| Team location | Co-located, distributed domestic, or distributed across time zones | Distributed teams require deliberate communication design affecting tooling, meeting cadence, and async documentation |
| Team maturity | Experience level and prior working relationship within the delivery team | A new team on a complex engagement is a compounding risk; maturity below the complexity level of the work is a flag for additional Delivery Owner attention |
| Leadership strength | Capability and availability of the engagement's delivery lead | Leadership strength is the highest-leverage variable on this list; a strong leader absorbs complexity and uncertainty that would otherwise surface as escalations |
| Domain knowledge | Degree to which the team holds relevant industry or environment knowledge | Low domain knowledge increases ramp-up time and the risk of incorrect assumptions; compensate with closer client involvement in early delivery phases |
| Dependencies | Number and nature of dependencies on third parties or client teams | A high dependency count increases schedule risk and reduces the Delivery Owner's control over outcomes; each dependency requires an identified owner and a fallback position |

### Uncertainty Variables

| Variable | What it measures | Planning implication |
|---|---|---|
| Market uncertainty | Degree to which external conditions could affect the engagement | High uncertainty warrants shorter planning horizons, more frequent replanning checkpoints, and explicit client alignment on how context changes will be handled |
| Technical uncertainty | Degree to which the technical approach is proven versus experimental | High uncertainty increases the value of spikes and proof-of-concept phases before full commitment; reflect in forecast confidence rating from day one |
| Number of stakeholders | Client-side stakeholders with a meaningful interest in the outcome | A high count increases alignment effort, lengthens decision cycles, and raises the risk of conflicting requirements surfacing mid-delivery |
| Duration | Planned length of the engagement | Longer engagements carry compounding uncertainty; require more frequent reforecast cycles and explicit attention to team continuity |
| Level of change control | Formality of the agreed change control process | Low change control in a high-complexity or high-uncertainty engagement is a material risk; agree a minimum change governance approach with the client before kickoff |

### Delivery Vehicle Indicators

| Profile shape | Likely delivery vehicle |
|---|---|
| High complexity / Low uncertainty | Structured project with detailed upfront plan and formal change control |
| Low complexity / High uncertainty | Advisory or consulting model with defined review points and flexible scope |
| High complexity / High uncertainty | Phased program with go/no-go gates; senior Delivery Owner required |
| Low complexity / Low uncertainty | Lightweight engagement model; standard managed service or short-form project |

### Pre-Signature Profiling on Threshold Deals

For deals above a threshold set jointly by the Delivery Director and Sales Lead — based on deal size, strategic risk, or a novel delivery model — this same profiling exercise runs before signature, during solution design, rather than waiting for Gate 1. At Gate 1 the profile can only inform mobilisation; run during solution design, it can still inform pricing and contractual terms while they are still being decided. On these threshold deals, the practitioner who completes the pre-signature profile is expected to become the engagement's Delivery Owner. This is an accountability, not a preference. An exception — where the profiler cannot take the engagement — is a deliberate decision made by the Delivery Director, not a default outcome of scheduling or availability.

The reasoning is straightforward: an assessment made by the person who will live with it is a different quality of assessment than one made for someone else. Knowing the profile is your own forecast, that you will be the one running the engagement against it, changes what you look for and how honestly you rate what you find. The profile stops being a form completed on someone else's behalf and becomes a personal forecast the profiler is accountable to. Gate 1 re-confirmation then stops being a compliance check and becomes a genuine question: do I still believe my own assessment.

Significant pre-signature involvement is real engagement work, not free work that happens before the engagement officially exists. Where it is material, it counts toward the Delivery Owner's complexity-weighted engagement load ceiling (see delivery-portfolio-management.md, Flow Management > Managed Queues > Delivery Owner engagement load) in the same way any other active commitment does, rather than sitting outside the portfolio's view of that person's capacity.

This is the same instrument used at Gate 1, run earlier for the deals where the timing matters most — not a separate exercise. A pre-signature profile is re-confirmed, not redone from scratch, at Gate 1: the Delivery Owner checks whether anything material changed between solution design and signature and updates the record accordingly.

### Solution Design Accountability on Threshold Deals

The framework has, until now, had no named owner for the quality of the solution design itself — the technical approach, the team shape, and the estimate that the price is built on. On the same threshold deals identified above, a Practice or Technical Lead co-signs the solution design before the proposal is submitted, confirming that the technical approach is sound, the team shape and estimate are credible, and the assumptions underlying the estimate are recorded rather than left implicit. The title used here — Practice or Technical Lead — reflects that in some organisations solution design soundness is owned by a Technical Lead rather than the Practice Lead named elsewhere in this framework; it does not rename that existing role.

At engagement closure, estimate-vs-actual variance on these deals is reviewed at the existing quarterly Alignment health check (see delivery-portfolio-management.md, Sales and Delivery Alignment > Cadence) rather than a new meeting. This gives the estimating function its own calibration loop, distinct from the Deal Quality at Handover commercial-position rating reviewed in the same session: under-estimation (the technical approach or team shape was wrong) and under-pricing (the estimate was right but the price did not reflect it) are different failures with different owners, and folding them into one number would hide which one is actually happening.

### Re-Profiling Triggers

The Gate 1 profile is a point-in-time judgement, not a permanent classification. The assumptions underneath it — sponsor stability, technical approach, scope stability — can break during delivery. Any of the following triggers a re-run of the profiling exercise:

- The client sponsor changes
- A Recovery and Exception Review is invoked (see Stage 2: Recovery and Exception Management)
- Scope or duration extends materially beyond the original estimate

The Delivery Owner and Practice Lead jointly own re-profiling, in the same way they owned the original exercise. It produces an updated composite profile and, where the profile shape has changed, a revised delivery vehicle recommendation. Both are logged as a dated entry in the Engagement Health Register (see Stage 2) alongside the Gate 1 record, rather than overwriting it — the framework needs visibility of how the engagement's shape has moved, not just where it started or where it currently sits.

This is not a heavier mechanism than the original profiling. It is the same exercise, re-run when the evidence says the assumptions underneath it no longer hold.

## Exit Criteria (Gate 1)

All of the following must be confirmed before the engagement moves to Stage 2:

- A Delivery Owner has been assigned and briefed
- The client sponsor and key stakeholders have been identified and mapped
- The SOW or engagement brief has been reviewed by the Delivery Owner
- Revenue recognition method and schedule have been agreed with Finance
- A kickoff date and pre-kickoff client meeting have been scheduled
- Assumptions and exclusions from the sale have been documented and flagged
- The Delivery Handover Record is complete, including the deal to delivery vehicle profile and the Deal Quality at Handover score
- Where conditions were attached to acceptance, they are documented in the Handover Record with owners and dates
- The team composition brief has been prepared and is ready to share with the client
