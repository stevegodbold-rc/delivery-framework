# Stage 2: Project, Engagement, and Consulting Delivery

## Purpose

Maintain visibility, support the workforce, and keep client relationships healthy across the full delivery lifecycle. The framework's role is not to run the delivery but to create the conditions for it to succeed.

---

## Entry Criteria (Gate 1 passed)

- All Gate 1 exit criteria have been confirmed
- The Delivery Handover Record is complete
- The team composition brief has been shared with the client
- Kickoff has been scheduled or completed

---

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Delivery Owner | Overall accountability for engagement health, client relationship, and team |
| Project Manager / Lead Consultant | Day-to-day delivery management within the chosen methodology |
| Practice Lead | Workforce planning, skills escalation, and delivery quality support |
| Finance Partner | Tracks actuals against forecast, flags revenue risk |
| Resource Manager | Manages allocation across engagements, surfaces utilisation risks |
| Delivery Director | Holds decision authority for Recovery and Exception reviews above the Delivery Owner's band; approves commercial concessions, scope renegotiation, write-offs, or early termination up to their own band, escalating above it to the Managing Director |

## Key Activities

**Fortnightly delivery health check.** The Delivery Owner reviews the engagement health score across all five dimensions and records a written rationale for each rating. Amber or Red dimensions trigger a conversation, not an escalation process. The review is logged in the Engagement Health Register.

**Engagement health scoring.** Each active engagement carries a health score reviewed fortnightly. It is a conversation prompt, not a performance grade.

| Dimension | What it measures | Leading indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Scope confidence | How well-defined is remaining scope | Scope change frequency, open change requests |
| Forecast confidence | Reliability of remaining timeline and cost estimate | Estimate revision frequency, dependency blockers |
| Relationship health | Quality of client sponsor engagement | Meeting attendance, response time, sentiment |
| Workforce fit | Whether the right people are on the engagement | Skill gap flags, utilisation variance |
| Commercial health | Whether revenue is tracking to plan | Actuals vs forecast, billing milestone status |

Each dimension is scored Green / Amber / Red by the Delivery Owner with a brief written rationale.

- Amber overall: any single dimension is Amber
- Red overall: any dimension is Red, or two or more are Amber

This rollup is a default, not a verdict. The Delivery Owner may override the computed overall rating with a documented rationale, for example where a single Amber dimension is already resolved or does not reflect material risk to the engagement. The rollup exists to prompt a conversation, not to replace the judgement of the person closest to the engagement.

**Vehicle-specific leading indicators.** Fixed-price and T&M engagements fail in different shapes, so the Forecast confidence and Commercial health dimensions are fed by different evidence depending on delivery vehicle. A self-assessed Amber rating is not sufficient on its own for either vehicle; it must be supported by the relevant check below.

- **Fixed-price engagements.** The Delivery Owner completes a cost-to-complete / estimate-at-completion (EAC) check at least monthly, and fortnightly once the engagement is Amber or Red, comparing remaining contract budget against a revised estimate of the cost to complete remaining scope. This check feeds the Forecast confidence dimension directly. It exists because an unfavourable EAC trend can be masked by a self-assessed rating for months if nothing forces the recalculation; the check is the evidence, the rating is the conversation prompt.
- **T&M engagements.** The Delivery Owner and Finance Partner track a funding / PO runway indicator — the number of days of confirmed, funded work remaining on the engagement — reviewed fortnightly, in the same way bench time is tracked for practitioners. This feeds the Commercial health dimension. A shortening runway with no renewal in active discussion is a trigger condition for Recovery and Exception Management, below, in its own right, independent of the health rating.

**Monthly client business review.** A structured conversation with the client sponsor covering relationship health, delivery progress, risks, and forward planning. Frequency may be adjusted by agreement with the client for shorter engagements.

**Scope and change management.** This framework does not define a change control process, as that sits within the project management methodology. It does require at a framework level that all agreed scope changes are reflected in an updated delivery forecast, that the Finance Partner is notified of any change affecting revenue recognition timing or value, and that changes implied during the sale but not captured in the SOW are flagged to the Sales Lead. Requests actioned informally, outside change control — additional work agreed verbally or "while you're at it" — still consume delivery capacity and must be included in the fixed-price cost-to-complete check above even where no formal change record exists. The absence of a change record is not evidence that no cost was incurred.

**Workforce management.** The Delivery Owner and Resource Manager monitor allocation and utilisation across the engagement. Elasticity signals are reviewed at the monthly workforce review: unresolved resource gaps, practitioners on bench for more than 10 business days, skills required but not held, and certifications appearing in bids but not in the team. Practitioners are not reallocated without a direct conversation with their Delivery Owner and the individual concerned.

**Review cadence.**

| Review | Frequency | Participants | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery health check | Fortnightly | Delivery Owner, Practice Lead | Review health score, surface risks early |
| Client business review | Monthly (or as agreed) | Delivery Owner, client sponsor | Relationship health, forward planning |
| Workforce review | Monthly | Practice Lead, Resource Manager | Utilisation, skilling, allocation gaps |
| Portfolio review | Monthly | All Delivery Owners, leadership | Cross-portfolio risk, capacity, and forecast |
| Exception Review | Within 5 business days of a trigger condition; every 2 weeks thereafter while open | Delivery Director, Delivery Owner, Finance Partner, Practice Lead (as needed) | Decide on commercial concessions, scope renegotiation, write-off, or early termination |

## Recovery and Exception Management

Most engagements never need this section. It exists for the minority that move from at risk to in trouble, so that a Red rating has somewhere to go beyond sitting in the Engagement Health Register with nobody empowered to act on it.

**Trigger conditions.** Any one of the following moves an engagement into Recovery and Exception status:

- Red overall health rating sustained across two consecutive fortnightly reviews
- A cost-to-complete / estimate-at-completion check shows remaining budget forecast to cover less than 80% of the revised cost to complete remaining scope, for a fixed-price engagement (this default threshold may be tightened by the Delivery Director for higher-risk engagements, but not relaxed by the Delivery Owner)
- The client signals an intention to reduce funding, reduce scope, or not renew a PO, for a T&M engagement
- The Delivery Owner or Finance Partner requests an Exception Review directly, regardless of whether a formal threshold has been crossed

**Convening.** An Exception Review is convened when a trigger condition above is met, or when a decision is needed that sits above the Delivery Owner's band in the delegation table (see delivery-portfolio-management.md, Delegation of Authority). The Delivery Owner notifies the Delivery Director within 2 business days of a trigger condition being met, or raises the need for a decision above their band directly. The Delivery Director convenes an Exception Review within 5 business days of notification, with the Finance Partner and, where there are workforce implications, the Practice Lead. This is an internal decision forum and precedes, rather than replaces, the client conversation that follows from it.

**Decision rights.** Decision rights for commercial concessions, write-offs, scope renegotiation, and early termination follow the three-band delegation structure set out in delivery-portfolio-management.md, Delegation of Authority: a Delivery Owner may approve a commercial concession or write-off within the lowest band directly, without convening an Exception Review, provided it is logged in the Engagement Health Register. Above that band, the Delivery Director holds explicit authority, above the Delivery Owner, to:

- Approve a commercial concession (discount, fee cap, or absorbed cost)
- Approve a renegotiated scope, including a documented partial delivery
- Approve a write-off of unrecoverable cost
- Approve early termination of the engagement

Above the Delivery Director's band, or for a strategic account, these decisions escalate to the Managing Director, with the Finance Partner informing the decision. The commercial value thresholds that define each band are set annually from the business plan, not by this framework (see Delegation of Authority).

This authority sits with the Delivery Director because these decisions carry commercial and relationship consequences beyond what a single Delivery Owner should be expected to carry alone, and because a Delivery Owner mid-engagement has a structural incentive to keep a rating at Amber rather than call it Red. The Exception Review takes that judgement call out of one person's hands without removing the Delivery Owner's day-to-day accountability for the engagement.

**Output.** Every Exception Review produces a written Exception Decision Record: the trigger, the decision taken, the rationale, any commercial or scope changes agreed, and a follow-up review date. It is logged alongside the Engagement Health Register entry. Where the decision changes contracted deliverables or the engagement's end date, it feeds the alternate Gate 2 route below.

An engagement in Recovery and Exception status remains under two-weekly Delivery Director review until it returns to Green or Amber, or reaches an agreed early-termination or partial-closure outcome.

## Methodology Interfaces

The framework sits above delivery methodology; it does not replace it. Its governance rhythm — the fortnightly health check, the monthly client business review, the Gates, and the Recovery and Exception triggers above — consumes methodology outputs as evidence. It does not replace or duplicate them. Where a framework cadence conflicts with a client-mandated or team-native methodology cadence, the methodology's cadence is recognised as authoritative and the framework's check is fitted around it.

How that plays out depends on the delivery vehicle established at Gate 1 (see Stage 1: Deal to Delivery Vehicle Profiling).

**Structured project (Waterfall/PRINCE2-style).** Client-mandated stage boundaries stand in place of an internally invented cadence. The fortnightly health check references the methodology's own status artefacts — a Highlight Report, a RAID log — as evidence, rather than re-deriving a parallel view. Where the client runs a Project Board, representation follows the delegation table in delivery-portfolio-management.md (Delegation of Authority): a senior Delivery Owner, not the engagement's own, represents the firm while the engagement is Green or Amber; the Delivery Director represents the firm directly once the engagement is Red-status or is a threshold engagement. The Recovery and Exception Review feeds into Board reporting; it does not run alongside it as a separate track.

**Advisory or consulting model.** These engagements typically carry no heavy methodology of their own. In practice, the monthly client business review often functions as the primary governance point. Given the engagement's lower complexity profile, the fortnightly internal health check can run at reduced formality — a brief written rationale against each dimension is sufficient evidence. This is a right-sizing of the check, not licence to skip it.

**Phased program with go/no-go gates.** Each phase's go/no-go decision is an internal checkpoint the fortnightly health check feeds into, timed to land before the decision rather than after it. The Recovery and Exception trigger conditions above apply at the phase level as well as the whole-engagement level: a program can have one healthy phase masking a troubled one, and phase-level triggers catch that before it surfaces in the aggregate rating.

**Lightweight engagement / managed service (typically Scrum or Kanban).** The fortnightly health check consumes the team's own ceremony outputs as its evidence base: sprint review, retrospective, and velocity/burndown trend for Scrum teams; cycle time and WIP data for Kanban teams. The Delivery Owner's rating is drawn from what the team already tracks, not formed independently of it. This exists to prevent a shadow reporting channel the team does not recognise or trust.

## Exit Criteria (Gate 2)

All of the following must be confirmed before the engagement moves to Stage 3:

- All contracted deliverables are completed or formally accepted by the client
- Outstanding scope items, exceptions, or carve-outs are documented and agreed in writing with the client
- A client closure meeting date has been scheduled with the client sponsor
- The Finance Partner has confirmed the final billing position
- The final health register entry has been completed and reflects the closing state of the engagement

### Alternate Route: Early Termination or Partial Closure

Where an engagement ends before contracted scope is complete — through client-initiated termination, a Recovery and Exception decision, or mutual agreement — Gate 2 is passed via this route instead of the criteria above:

- A documented reason for the early end has been recorded, including whether it originated from the client, from a Recovery and Exception decision, or from mutual agreement
- A client-agreed statement of what was delivered and what was not has been produced and shared with the client
- A revised commercial settlement has been agreed and confirmed by the Finance Partner, reflecting the Exception Decision Record where one was taken
- The Delivery Director has signed off the early termination or partial closure via the Exception Decision Record
- A client closure meeting date has been scheduled, following the same Stage 3 process as a completed engagement

This route does not relax accountability. It substitutes, for completeness, the things a clean engagement would have had by default: a documented reason, an agreed record of what was actually delivered, and a settled commercial position.
